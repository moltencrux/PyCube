# Derivation of Time to Impact from Height $h$ with Initial Velocity $v_0$

## Given:
- Acceleration due to gravity: $g$
- Initial velocity: $v_0$
- Initial height: $h$

## Step 1: Use the kinematic equation for displacement
The displacement under constant acceleration is:

$$ h = v_0 t + \frac{1}{2} g t^2 $$

## Step 2: Rearrange into standard quadratic form

$$ \frac{1}{2} g t^2 + v_0 t - h = 0 $$
Multiply through by 2 to simplify:

$$ g t^2 + 2 v_0 t - 2 h = 0 $$

## Step 3: Solve the quadratic equation
Using the quadratic formula $t = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{2 a}$, where:
- $a = g$
- $b = 2 v_0$
- $c = -2 h$

The solution is:

$$ t = \frac{-2 v_0 \pm \sqrt{(2 v_0)^2 + 8 g h}}{2 g} $$

## Step 4: Select the positive root
Since time must be positive, we take the positive root:

$$ t = \frac{-2 v_0 + \sqrt{4 v_0^2 + 8 g h}}{2 g} $$

Simplify the expression:

$$ t = \frac{-v_0 + \sqrt{v_0^2 + 2 g h}}{g} $$

## Final Answer:
The time $t$ to impact from height $h$ with initial velocity $v_0$ is:

$$ \boxed{t = \frac{-v_0 + \sqrt{v_0^2 + 2 g h}}{g}} $$
